01
Base Camouflage
USAAF Spec 24114 · Sept 1940
The factory-applied two-tone scheme on most ETO fighters before mid-1944. Olive Drab faded dramatically in service — heavy panel-tone variation is historically correct, not careless. Use this as the dominant body color of each mini before applying Group/Squadron markings.
02
Group Nose Schemes
VIII Fighter Command · March 1944
Each Fighter Group adopted a distinctive nose scheme — solid color, checkers, stripes, or diamonds — for at-a-glance formation identification. Pick one for the whole Kill Team to read as a single squadron. Click any card for paint specs.
03
Squadron Rudder Colors
8th AF Order · Fall 1944
Within each Group, individual squadrons were identified by rudder color. For the Kill Team this is the per-mini differentiator — use it on a helmet faceplate, chest panel, knee pad, or backpack vent. Pick a different rudder color per mini to give each pilot their own identity.
04
Trim & Personal Markings
Pilot & Crew Chief Discretion
The top layer — pilot personalization. Script names, kill tallies, nose pinups, mission marks. Almost always applied on the LEFT (port) side of the cowling for the press cameras.
05
Weathering & Effect Colors
For Oils, Washes & Exhaust Staining
The colors you'll reach for after the base scheme is down — exhaust stains, oil leaks, gun blast, chipped aluminum, sun-faded OD highlights. These tie the WWII modeling techniques together on top of standard 40k washes.
